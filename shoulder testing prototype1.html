Step 2 · Static posture

Standing posture, front and side

Two 3-second captures. The app measures shoulder and head alignment from detected joint centres and averages them over about 75 frames.

Step 3 · Active range of motion

Shoulder AROM, both sides

Test the unaffected side first to give a personal reference. The patient presses the pain button (or the space bar) when pain starts and again when it stops, which records a painful arc.

Step 4 · Camera-assisted tests

Modified special tests

Grouped by structure for differential reasoning: rotator cuff, subacromial, biceps and labrum, AC joint. These are remote adaptations. The camera confirms the test position and measures speed, hold time and reach; the patient reports pain and weakness. The report labels them as modified and counts the published test clusters for reference.

Step 5 · Patient-reported outcomes

Pain, SPADI, ASES and patient global

The SPADI pain and disability subscales match the OMERACT shoulder pain and function domains most closely, so it stays the primary measure. ASES adds a cuff-oriented function score; the patient global and PSFS cover what matters most to this patient.

Numeric pain rating (0 = no pain, 10 = worst imaginable)

SPADI

ASES shoulder score, patient section

American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons standardised assessment. Recommended alongside the DASH for rotator cuff and subacromial conditions. Rate the affected shoulder. 0–100, higher is better.

Patient global and patient-specific function

Patient global assessment is one of the four mandatory domains in the OMERACT core domain set for shoulder disorders. The Patient-Specific Functional Scale records up to three activities the patient chooses.
